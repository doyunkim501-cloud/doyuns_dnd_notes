Basic Gameplay:

  A group of players embody their characters, guided by (typically) a single Game/Dungeon Master. 

 Each party has its own dynamic- typically expect to have in-character as well as out-of-character conversations. 

 Always keep track of what your character would do. My personal rule for DND is that it’s a role playing game, and if you can stay in character for major decisions as much as possible, it is best. 

Use the Alignment System to guide your character’s actions. 

[https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/dungeonsdragons/images/d/d2/Alignment-chart.jpg/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/900?cb=20200419002403](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/dungeonsdragons/images/d/d2/Alignment-chart.jpg/revision/latest/scale-to-width-down/900?cb=20200419002403)

The Background you take can help guide your character- though these don’t have to be strict rules to follow. 

There is something called the action economy. Everything you can do or say has an action “cost” associated with it. You get one of each action type. Everything is measured in 6 second increments (at least during combat, and during certain key moments). The types of actions, and examples of them are:

Action

An attack, a major environment interaction (e.g. picking a lock), a major character interaction (e.g. giving healing potions, casting (most) spells.

Bonus Action

Minor environment interactions (e.g. opening a large door), minor character interactions (e.g. passing an item to someone else), casting (some) spells. 

Reaction

 These are specific things you can do when something triggers an action marked “Reaction,” such as hitting someone when they run past you, casting certain spells. 

Movement

 Your base movement is determined by both your Race and Class (or Sub-Class in some cases). Each Race has its own movement, measured in feet per 6 seconds. Certain class abilities allow you to increase your movement. 

Note: Certain abilities/feats (bonus abilities you can get) can change an action type (e.g. Rogues are able to take “hide” as a bonus action, when it’s normally a full action). 

Your Stats will determine how effective you are at some of these action types- determined through dice roll (d20). 

 Dice will be referred to as “d<NUMBER>.” The number indicates the amount of sides the dice has. A “prefix” number to the “d” will tell you how many of each die to roll. The common dice types in DnD are:

D4 (4 sided)

D6 (6 sided)

D8 (8 sided)

D10 (10 sided)

D12 (12 sided)

D20 (20 sided; this is most common)
  

Skills, Stats, Saves:

 Each character will have 6 characteristics/Stats governing their abilities. These are: 

Strength (STR): how physically strong you are 

Dexterity (DEX): how physically adroit you are

Constitution (CON): how physically (and magically) healthy you are

Intelligence (INT): how smart you are

Wisdom (WIS): how wise you are

Charisma (CHA): how charismatic you are

Every Race has different increases to Stats, typically 2 stats of choice (+2 to a primary and +1 to a secondary stat). As well, due to the different abilities of each Class and sub-class, they will benefit from having specific stats be higher than others. 

A 10 in a stat means that you have 0 benefit from it (neutral). This is what the average person in a Dungeons and Dragons world will have for all stats (10s, 10s, 10s across the board).

A stat can range from 1 to 20, ordinarily. A stat reaching 0 is fatal in some cases (e.g. Con). You will determine your stats by: 

Roll 4d6 

Subtract the LOWEST NUMBER

Do this 6 times, and assign the Stats as you see fit. 

Add in your racial bonuses. 

You can only increase a Stat by: 

Leveling up and reaching a specific milestone (every 4 levels, typically)

You can take +2 in ONE Stat, or +1 in TWO Stats. 

You can also choose to ignore the Stat up, and take a Feat instead, which can significantly impact how your character plays. 

 Finding a magic item which increases your Stat magically

This can increase your Stat beyond 20, but it usually doesn’t last forever. 
 
 There are many Stats (marked on your character sheet) that you will refer to while making roleplay or combat rolls. These are determined by your Stat. 

 You will add a certain number (or subtract) to a d20 roll to find your skill roll. This number is your ability modifier (Stat Score - 10, divide by 2, and round down). Your character sheet will typically automatically calculate this. 

Stats will also determine how lucky you are when you roll Saves. 

Your Save is calculated by adding your Modifier to your d20 roll (e.g. if I have a 15 in STR, my modifier is 2, and my STR saving roll with a 12 on d20 roll is 12 + 2 = 14). 

 Weapon skills (attacks) require an Attack Roll (d20 + ability modifier + proficiency, if you have it). If it successfully hits, then you can calculate your damage roll (weapon dice, anywhere from d4 to d12) + ability modifier. 

 If you have a magic weapon, you get to add its bonus to both the attack roll AND the damage roll.
  

  

Proficiencies:

Every character is proficient in a set of skills and saves. This is determined by 1) your starting class, 2) the character background you choose. 

You will add your Proficiency Bonus (from level 1-4, this is +2) to skill checks and saving throws to skills and saving throws that you have Proficiency in (marked by starting class and background). 

You will also have Proficiency with certain weapons - this means that you can add this Bonus to Attack Rolls you make (d20 roll + relevant Stat + Proficiency). 

 Your Proficiency Bonus will go up as you level up. For specific amounts, see: [https://roll20.net/compendium/dnd5e/Character%20Advancement#content](https://roll20.net/compendium/dnd5e/Character%20Advancement#content)



Making a Character:

There are many places to start making a character. Think about the kind of story you want to tell and live out. Will your character be fierce and strong? Will they be cowardly? Briefly look through the description of each starting class and Race (check with the dungeon master about how each Race will work in the setting), and put together a rough idea of how your character will inhabit the world. 
  
  Once you have a starting Class and Race, roll for your 6 stats! 
 
  Remember to roll 4d6 and subtract your lowest roll out of each set. 

 You can assign the stats after rolling all of them. Read through your Class notes in detail now, in the Player Handbook, and see what stats will be the most important. Don’t forget to look ahead at sub-classes, as sometimes you’ll need a stat that might not normally be high for your class! 

Finally, add in your racial bonuses.

 After getting your stats, fill in your character’s name, class, alignment, speed, weight, height, and Initiative. 

For weight and height, don’t worry too much- go with anything that makes sense to you after reading the Race description in the Player Handbook. Weight has gameplay impact only by affecting your carry weight (and Doyun doesn’t really keep track of it until it’s really ridiculous for one character to carry). 

Speed is determined by your Race (Short Races will move slower (25ft/6 sec), Medium Races will move at average speeds (30ft/6sec); Class bonuses and magic items will increase movement, sometimes). 

Initiative is your Dexterity Modifier (e.g. if your Dex is 16, your Mod is 3; your Initiative is 3). 

This is used to determine who goes first in time-sensitive situations, typically in Combat. It is a modifier you add to a d20 roll.

 Write down your starting items. Each Class is allowed a set of starting items, as specified by the Class description in the Player Handbook. Alternately, you can use your Starting Gold (if provided by a dungeon master) to buy your starting gear (costs are outlined in the Player Handbook). Sometimes you will have starting equipment AND starting gold to spend.

Take note of what you will need for combat and roleplay. Try to take weapons and items you have proficiency in, though it can also be fun to roll without proficiency sometimes. 

Determine your Background (listed at the back of the Player Handbook). This will grant you some extra gold, as well as additional Skill and tool Proficiencies. 

This is a good time to mark down all your Proficiencies! Rogues and Bards get bonuses to some skills, so don’t forget to make note of which skills have these bonuses, if you’re playing a Rogue or a Bard. 

 Make sure that your Proficiency Bonus is noted! 

Roll for your Health (HP). 

This is determined by your “hit dice.” Each Class has a dice associated with it (from d6 to d12) that defines your HP. 

At first level, you take the maximum value of your Hit Die and add your CON modifier to it. If your CON mod is negative (fairly common), subtract the modifier from your Hit Die. 

After, roll your Hit Die and add your CON mod to it. 

For instance, if I am making a 3rd level character that uses d8 hit die, and my CON mod is +2, I start with 10 HP. I’ll roll 2d8 for my level ups (e.g. 10), and add my CON mod twice (+4), to get +14 HP. My HP for this 3rd level character is now 24.

Certain Feats will allow you to increase your HP when leveling up. 

If you level up your CON score and your modifier increases as well, the change is retroactive; add the difference  (e.g. +1) per previous level up, starting from 1st level.

 Calculate your Armor Class (AC)

This determines how hard it is to hit you. 
 
 When you’re naked, or have no armor on at all, your AC is 10 + DEX mod. 

Typically your armor will determine your AC (the Player Handbook armor list will tell you how to calculate it). 
 
 Some classes will have a higher AC without wearing armor (monks and barbarians). 
 
 If you have a spell-casting class, make sure to indicate your spellcasting attack modifier (proficiency bonus + relevant stat mod) and your spell save “DC” (8 + prof bonus + stat mod). 

Spell attack modifiers are used for spells that require you to roll to make an attack (e.g. Firebolt), and work the same way as weapon attacks. 

Spell save “DC” sets a difficulty check for your target. If your target passes, your spell has no effect (or reduced); if your target fails, your spell has full effect. 

 Set your languages. Almost every character will communicate in Common - the universal language of every DND land. Based on your race and Background, you will understand more languages. 
 
 This can have a major impact on how your character can interact with the world! 

If you do have Feats, or choose to take Feats on leveling up, make sure to note them down. The list of Feats: [https://5e.tools/feats.html#aberrant%20dragonmark_erlw](https://5e.tools/feats.html#aberrant%20dragonmark_erlw)

 If you are a spell caster, make sure to refer to the Player Handbook to determine how many spells you know and can cast (and look online for what spells you CAN take). Each casting class works a little differently.
